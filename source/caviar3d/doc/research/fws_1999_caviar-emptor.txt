Press Release: Caviar Emptor - Additional Trade Controls Benefit Wild Sturgeon Conservation and Consumers

Effective December 6, 1999, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service will no longer issue or accept certificates for caviar predating April 1, 1998, under CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species).

This regulation strengthens wild sturgeon protection. The 146 CITES member nations added all sturgeon and paddlefish species to CITES Appendix II as of April 1, 1998, due to increasing international demand for Caspian Sea sturgeon. Since then, caviar imports have required CITES permits verifying legal acquisition and ensuring trade doesn't jeopardize wild sturgeon survival.

The prohibition offers consumer benefits as well. According to the sturgeon products industry, "the normal shelf life for caviar is 12 months." Since caviar imported before April 1998 would have exceeded its shelf life by December 6, 1999, the regulation ensures consumers receive fresher product while supporting conservation efforts.

The rules do not apply to aquaculture-produced caviar or wild caviar harvested after April 1, 1998, which remain permitted with valid CITES documentation. Commercial trade in two endangered sturgeon species (shortnose and Baltic) had been banned since 1975.

Individual travelers may legally transport 250 grams of caviar per person per trip for personal consumption.
